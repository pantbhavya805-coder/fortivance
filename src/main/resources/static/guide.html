1. Start here

Banks run on outside companies: cloud providers, payment processors, SMS gateways. When one of them fails, the bank has to work out quickly which customer services are hit, whether it must tell the regulator, and what to do. And it must be able to prove, at any time, that each important vendor is properly controlled.

Fortivance helps with both jobs:

  • Incidents: it reads a vendor's incident email, traces which bank services are affected and how badly, starts the regulator's 6-hour clock, and drafts a response plan for a person to approve.
  • Evidence: it reads vendor contracts and audit reports, checks them against the regulator's requirements, shows each gap with the exact clause, and scores how much risk is left.
Judging this project? The Judge guide has the sign-in details, a demo path, and how Fortivance meets the hackathon checklist and criteria.

Suggested 5-minute path for judges: read section 2 (the setting), open Incidents and upload NH-INC-2026-0412.md from sample-docs/incident-notices, follow section 3 alongside it, then open Evidence and follow section 4. Underlined terms anywhere in the app explain themselves when you hover or tap them, and the Guide for this page button opens the matching section of this guide.

Everything in the demo is synthetic: the bank, the vendors, their contracts and their incidents are invented, but they are modelled on real Indian banking rules.

2. The setting

Deccan Meridian Bank (DMB) is a fictional commercial bank in Hyderabad. It is regulated by the RBI (Reserve Bank of India), which expects banks to manage the risk of outsourcing under the RBI Outsourcing Directions 2025, and to report significant cyber incidents within 6 hours. Managing vendor risk is called TPRM (Third-Party Risk Management); keeping services running through failures is operational resilience.

Business services, tolerances and continuity plans

For each business service the bank sets an impact tolerance: the longest the service may be down before the harm to customers becomes unacceptable. It also has a BCP (Business Continuity Plan): a pre-written playbook for keeping the service going, such as switching to a backup provider.

CodeBusiness serviceImpact toleranceContinuity plan
SVC-ALERTCustomer Alerts & OTP1 hourBCP-06
SVC-PAYDigital Payments (UPI/IMPS)2 hoursBCP-02
SVC-CARDCard Payments2 hoursBCP-03
SVC-ACCTRetail Accounts & Deposits4 hoursBCP-01
SVC-ONBCustomer Onboarding24 hoursBCP-04
SVC-LENDRetail Lending24 hoursBCP-05

Each service also records which applications it cannot work without (essential dependencies). That is what decides the impact level of an incident:

  • DISRUPTED: an essential application is hit, so the service is effectively down. Payments need OTPs, so a CNE failure disrupts UPI payments.
  • DEGRADED: only a non-essential application is hit; the service still works, but worse. Card payments only use CNE for alert messages, so cards are degraded.

These are the only two impact levels. "Exposed" means every service the incident reaches, disrupted or degraded.

In the demo this register is pre-loaded. A real bank gets its own Fortivance instance and loads its own vendors, systems and services from three Excel templates on the Import Vendor Register page (Dependency Graph → Import register).

3. Incidents: what happens and why

A vendor emails the bank: "we had a problem". The bank now has a few hours to answer three questions: what is actually broken for our customers, do we have to tell the regulator, and what do we do now?

Without a tool, an analyst reads the email, looks up which systems use that vendor in a spreadsheet, asks colleagues which services depend on those systems, and writes a plan, while the RBI 6-hour reporting window runs. Fortivance does the reading and tracing in minutes and leaves the decisions to people.

Worked example: NotifyHub SMS delays (NH-INC-2026-0412)

NotifyHub writes that between 09:10 and 14:30 IST some SMS messages, including OTPs, were delayed or lost, because of a problem at "one of our upstream partners" (TextLink).

  1. Trace: NotifyHub supports two bank applications: CNE (all OTPs and alerts) and MBB (mobile app login OTPs).
  2. Impact: CNE is essential for Alerts & OTP and for UPI payments, so both are DISRUPTED. Card payments and Retail accounts only use CNE or MBB for non-essential messages, so they are DEGRADED. Result: "2 disrupted / 4 exposed".
  3. Tolerance: the outage lasted about 5 hours 20 minutes. The tolerance for Alerts & OTP is 1 hour and for UPI payments 2 hours, so both were breached. This is what turns a vendor's "no action required" into a serious event for the bank.
  4. Hidden risk: the cause was TextLink, a fourth party that NotifyHub's contract never disclosed. The plan asks NotifyHub about it.
The vendor called it a minor, resolved delay. Fortivance shows that, for this bank, it breached the tolerance of two critical services. That gap between what the vendor says and what it means for the bank is the core of the incident module.

4. Vendor evidence: what happens and why

RBI expects a bank to prove, at any time, that each important vendor is properly controlled: that the contract gives the right protections and that independent audits back them up. "We trust them" does not count; a clause or an audit finding does. That proof is called evidence.

Without a tool, an analyst reads 50-page contracts and audit reports against a checklist by hand, once a year. Fortivance reads them in minutes, quotes the exact clause for each requirement, and shows the gaps.

What is checked: the requirements

Each requirement (code VS-…: vendor standard) is a control the bank expects, mostly taken from the RBI Outsourcing Directions 2025. The demo uses five:

CodeRequirementIn plain wordsSource
VS-CON-01Audit and regulator access rightsThe bank, its auditors and RBI can inspect the vendor and its subcontractors.RBI para 69(xii)
VS-CON-02Incident notification obligationThe vendor must tell the bank about incidents within a set time, to a named person.RBI para 56; 69(iii)
VS-CON-03Control over subcontractingThe vendor needs the bank's approval to subcontract important work, and its obligations pass down.RBI para 4(2); 69(i), (xiii)
VS-BCP-01Tested BCP/DRThe vendor has a continuity and disaster-recovery plan, tests it, and shares the results.RBI para 78
VS-SEC-02Subservice organisations coveredIf the vendor's audit report leaves out a subcontractor (a "carve-out"), the bank gets assurance over it some other way.Internal policy

CON = contract clause, BCP = business continuity, SEC = security assurance. Paragraph numbers marked "(verify)" in the app should be checked against the official RBI text before quoting.

The evidence screen

  1. Overview: one row per vendor with coverage (requirements with evidence FOUND, out of five), how many of those a person has verified, counts of FOUND / PARTIAL / MISSING, and the residual risk score.
  2. Gap report (View Gap Report): each requirement with its status, the RBI source and the human review state. Expand a row to see the exact passages quoted from the document, the AI's reasoning and its confidence, and the Verify / Reject buttons.
  3. Evidence pack (Export): a printable version of the gap report with citations, for auditors or the board.

Out of scope: RBI's IT outsourcing rules do not apply to SMS gateways (para 5(2)(iii)), so NotifyHub gets no contract gap check. It still gets a risk score and is monitored for incidents and continuity. PayRail does not appear because the demo has no contract or audit report on file for it.

How the risk score works

The residual risk score (0 to 100) shows how much risk is left after counting what the bank can prove is under control. It starts with the vendor's inherent risk and adds every gap that is not covered:

FactorPointsWhy it adds risk
Risk tierCRITICAL 40, HIGH 30, MEDIUM 20, LOW 10How much of the bank depends on the vendor
Data accessFinancial 15, customer personal data 10, internal 5How much harm a breach would cause
Missing evidence8 per requirement (max 30)A control you cannot prove gives no protection
Partial evidence4 per requirement (max 16)Only partly proven
Periodic review overdue10Oversight has lapsed
Open incidents in the vendor's chain5 each (max 10)Something is going wrong right now; drops off when closed
Subcontractors with no assurance on file5 eachA blind spot in the supply chain

70 and above is HIGH, 40 to 69 MEDIUM, below 40 LOW. Example: NimbusCloud = 40 (critical) + 15 (financial data) + 8 (VS-SEC-02 missing: its audit report carves out SecureVault) + 5 (open cooling incident) + 5 (SecureVault has no audit report on file) = 73, HIGH.

The checks follow the RBI Directions; the point weights are the bank's own settings, not an RBI formula. Because every point is listed with its reason, an analyst can explain the number to an auditor line by line.

5. Why it is built this way

  • AI reads and writes; code decides what must be exact. Claude extracts facts from emails, maps contract clauses and drafts plans. The dependency trace, impact levels, the clock, coverage and the risk score are ordinary, testable code.
  • People make the decisions. Every AI output is marked "needs review". Plans need an approver, and actions exist only after approval.
  • No regulatory verdicts. Whether to report to RBI or CERT-In is a legal judgement, so Fortivance shows the clock and lists the question, but never answers it.
  • Citations that can be checked. The AI may only cite passage IDs from the document; any citation that does not exist is discarded.
  • Untrusted input stays untrusted. Uploaded notices are treated as data. Hidden instructions are flagged and the plan is withheld.
  • Measured, not assumed. The Evaluation page scores the AI against hand-labelled gold data, including prompt-injection cases, and every AI call is logged with its cost.
